— SGN · 01 · N · MODELLUM —

Three days. Three weeks. Three months.

Oxygen is wine's quiet enemy. Even the finest seal cannot prevent what the molecule already inside the bottle will do over time. The nitrogen module addresses this directly: after the vacuum cycle removes ambient air, a measured pulse of food-grade N₂ floods the headspace, displacing residual oxygen to a fraction of one percent.

The result is the same atmosphere a cellar master uses to protect a barrel mid-fermentation — now contained within a 142-gram object that fits in your hand. A bottle opened tonight can be tasted, blind, against itself ninety days later. We have done it. The difference is not detectable.

How the nitrogen cycle works

— phase i —

Vacuum Draw

The mechanical pump removes 70% of headspace air through the silicone valve. Oxygen concentration drops from 21% to approximately 6%.

— phase ii —

N₂ Release

A 0.4-second pulse of pure nitrogen is released from the integrated capsule directly into the bottle's headspace through a precision micro-valve.

— phase iii —

Displacement

Nitrogen — heavier than oxygen at this concentration — settles onto the wine's surface, pushing the remaining oxygen up and through the exhaust valve.

— phase iv —

Inert Lock

The membrane closes. The bottle is now sealed beneath an atmosphere of 99.4% pure nitrogen — chemically incapable of oxidising the wine.

Why nitrogen, and not argon, CO₂, or simply more vacuum?

Nitrogen is the gas already present in 78% of the air we breathe. It is biologically inert, tasteless, odourless, and — critically — does not dissolve into wine the way carbon dioxide does. Argon is heavier and more effective in laboratory conditions, but its cost is prohibitive and its supply chain unreliable. Pure vacuum, beyond a certain threshold, begins to draw aromatic compounds out of the wine itself. Nitrogen alone offers preservation without intervention — it protects the wine without ever touching its character.

— The Engineering —

Three principles, one promise.

Preservation is not a feature. It is the entire reason these objects exist. Every line, every gram, every degree of rotation has been weighed against this one demand.

— I —

Vacuum Genesis

A miniature axial pump removes air molecules through a one-way silicone valve. Each rotation reduces internal pressure by 0.1 bar — a measurable absence rather than a marketed promise.

— II —

Tri-axis Lock

Three radial claws expand in unison beneath the bottle lip, distributing load across 360°. The result is an interlock that does not flex, does not creep, does not surrender to time or pressure.

— III —

Hermetic Membrane

A medical-grade silicone gasket compresses by exactly 0.4 mm under load — the precise tolerance at which a seal becomes hermetic without crushing the bottle's glass crown.

— IV —

Anodised Skin

Aerospace-grade aluminium passes through 47 minutes of electrochemical anodisation, producing a surface that resists scratching, fingerprints, and the patina of frequent handling.

— V —

Universal Geometry

Calibrated for the four dominant bottle-neck profiles — Bordelaise, Bourguignonne, Champenoise, and Hochbouteille — accommodating 98% of bottles produced globally without adapter or compromise.

— VI —

Mechanical Memory

No batteries. No firmware. No degradation. Pure mechanical engineering means the SIGNATUS opened in 2046 will function identically to the one you receive today — a tool, not a gadget.

— Considered Questions —

Answers, without artifice.

How long does SIGNATUS preserve an opened bottle?

+
In vacuum mode, still wines retain their full aromatic profile for up to ten days at proper cellar temperature. In nitrogen mode (SGN·01·N), the same wines retain their character for ninety days or longer — we have conducted blind panels confirming indistinguishable results at the three-month mark. Sparkling wines retain effervescence for up to five days under the Champagne Sealer. All figures are derived from controlled tasting panels, not marketing optimism.

Is the nitrogen safe? Will it affect the taste?

+
Entirely safe and entirely tasteless. Nitrogen makes up 78% of the air you are breathing right now — it is biologically inert, chemically stable, and approved under EU regulation 1333/2008 as food additive E941. It does not dissolve into the wine, does not interact with any aromatic compound, and does not alter texture, body, or finish. The wine you taste from a nitrogen-preserved bottle is, molecularly, the same wine you would have tasted on the night you opened it.

How long does one nitrogen capsule last?

+
Each food-grade N₂ capsule contains enough nitrogen for approximately thirty bottle preservations. The capsule is housed within the SIGNATUS body and is replaced in under ten seconds — no tools required. Replacement capsules ship in recyclable packs of six and are available through our atelier or any partnered Austrian Vinothek.

Does the vacuum or nitrogen affect the wine itself?

+
No. The vacuum operates above the liquid, removing only the headspace oxygen that drives oxidation. The nitrogen, similarly, occupies only the space between the wine surface and the seal. The wine itself is never subjected to negative pressure, never contacts the membrane or capsule, and is not altered in any chemical or sensory dimension.

What is the difference between SGN·01 and SGN·01·N?

+
The SGN·01 is the pure-vacuum model: a mechanical pump that removes air and seals the bottle for up to ten days. The SGN·01·N adds an integrated nitrogen module — after the vacuum cycle, a measured pulse of food-grade N₂ floods the headspace, displacing residual oxygen and extending preservation to ninety days or more. The bodies are externally identical; only the internal architecture differs. Both can be ordered with or without the matching Champagne Sealer (SGN·02).

Will it fit older or unusually shaped bottles?

+
The SIGNATUS is calibrated for the four dominant bottle-neck profiles — Bordelaise, Bourguignonne, Champenoise, and Hochbouteille (the traditional Austrian and German Riesling shape) — covering approximately 98% of bottles in production. Vintage bottles with non-standard necks should be tested individually; we provide a 30-day return window for incompatible fits.

Are the two products interchangeable?

+
No. The Wine Sealer creates negative pressure (and optionally nitrogen displacement), which is appropriate for still wines but would damage a sparkling wine bottle by drawing dissolved CO₂ out of solution. The Champagne Sealer holds positive pressure, which is essential for retaining bubbles. Each is designed for its own purpose. We recommend both.

How is SIGNATUS cleaned and maintained?

+
The silicone membrane is removable and dishwasher-safe. The aluminium body should be wiped with a soft cloth — never submerged. The mechanical interior contains no consumable parts beyond the nitrogen capsule and requires no service for the engineered lifetime of the object.

What does the founding edition include?

+
Each SIGNATUS arrives in a hand-assembled walnut presentation case, accompanied by a numbered certificate of authenticity, two N₂ capsules (for the SGN·01·N), a microfibre cleaning cloth, and a printed monograph documenting the development process from Vienna to Steyr. The founding edition is limited to 1,200 pieces per model.
